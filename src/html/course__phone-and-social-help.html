Phone & Social Help for Seniors

Smartphones and social apps can help you stay close to family, but the settings, notifications, pop-ups, passwords, and privacy choices can feel overwhelming. This course helps seniors and beginners use their phone and common social apps with more confidence, less fear, and better safety habits.

Phone and Tablet Basics

  • Practise touch gestures such as tapping, swiping, scrolling, long-pressing, pinching to zoom, and switching between apps.
  • Understand home screens, app icons, notifications, Control Center or quick settings, Wi‑Fi, Bluetooth, sound, brightness, and battery basics.
  • Adjust accessibility settings such as text size, display zoom, voice dictation, hearing options, and emergency contact features.

Contacts, Calls, and Text Messages

  • Add, edit, search, and organize contacts so important family members, doctors, neighbours, and services are easier to reach.
  • Practise phone calls, speakerphone, voicemail, missed calls, blocking unwanted callers, and recognizing suspicious caller ID patterns.
  • Send texts, photos, voice messages, links, emojis, and group messages while understanding what is private and what others can see.

Photos, Apps, and Everyday Tasks

  • Take photos, find them later, favourite important pictures, send photos to family, and avoid accidentally sharing too much.
  • Install, update, remove, and organize apps safely using the App Store or Google Play without clicking suspicious ads.
  • Use practical everyday tools such as calendar, reminders, maps, weather, camera, notes, QR codes, and voice dictation.

Social Apps and Family Sharing

  • Learn the basics of Facebook, WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, or other apps your family already uses.
  • Understand profiles, friends, messages, comments, reactions, posts, groups, and shared photos in plain language.
  • Practise joining family groups, viewing grandchildren’s photos, sending private messages, and avoiding public oversharing.

Privacy, Scams, and Safer Habits

  • Review privacy settings for contacts, location, photos, microphone, camera, and social media visibility.
  • Spot suspicious texts, fake prize messages, friend requests from strangers, romance scams, urgent payment requests, and fake tech-support warnings.
  • Build a simple safety rule: pause, do not share codes or money, verify through another channel, and ask for help before responding.

What You Should Feel More Confident Doing

Use your phone or tablet for everyday tasks with less frustration.

Call, text, manage contacts, and share photos more confidently.

Understand social apps enough to stay connected without oversharing.

Recognize suspicious messages and protect your privacy settings.
