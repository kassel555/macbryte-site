Computer Basics for Seniors

If your computer feels confusing, you are not alone. This beginner-friendly course helps you understand the parts of your computer, practise the skills you use every day, and build confidence without being rushed. Lessons can be adapted for Mac or Windows and taught in your Ottawa-area home or online.

Getting Comfortable With Your Computer

  • Identify the main parts of your computer, including the screen, keyboard, mouse or trackpad, ports, charging cable, and power button.
  • Understand the difference between the computer, the operating system, apps, files, folders, and the internet.
  • Learn what common words mean — desktop, dock, taskbar, window, menu, icon, update, download, and cloud — in plain language.

Mouse, Trackpad, and Keyboard Practice

  • Practise single-clicking, double-clicking, right-clicking, dragging, scrolling, selecting text, and using a cursor calmly.
  • Learn useful keyboard keys such as Return, Delete, Shift, Command or Control, Tab, Escape, and the space bar.
  • Use simple shortcuts for copy, paste, undo, search, print, zoom, and switching between windows when you are ready.

Finding and Organizing Files

  • Understand where documents, downloads, photos, and desktop files usually live on Mac and Windows computers.
  • Create folders for important areas such as family photos, banking documents, medical paperwork, recipes, travel, or taxes.
  • Rename files clearly, search for lost files, move items safely, and learn when to copy instead of delete.

Settings, Wi‑Fi, Printing, and Updates

  • Connect to Wi‑Fi, check battery and sound settings, adjust text size, and find accessibility options that make the computer easier to use.
  • Understand software updates, why they matter, and how to avoid fake update pop-ups or suspicious download prompts.
  • Practise common tasks such as printing, saving a PDF, opening attachments, and checking whether a printer or internet connection is working.

Simple Troubleshooting You Can Do Yourself

  • Learn what to try when a page freezes, sound stops working, Wi‑Fi disconnects, an app will not open, or something disappears from the screen.
  • Build a calm checklist: pause, read the message, avoid random clicking, save your work, restart if needed, and ask for help when unsure.
  • Know when a problem is safe to handle yourself and when it is better to call Macbryte, Apple, Microsoft, your internet provider, or a trusted family member.

What You Should Feel More Confident Doing

Use your mouse, keyboard, windows, menus, and basic settings with more confidence.

Find important files and organize them in a way that makes sense to you.

Recognize common computer problems and know the first safe steps to try.

Feel calmer asking questions because you understand the basic vocabulary.

Common Questions

Is this course only for seniors?

No. It is designed for seniors and beginners, but anyone who wants patient, plain-language computer help can take it.

Can I learn on my own computer?

Yes. Learning on your own Mac or Windows computer is best because the buttons, settings, files, and apps match what you will use every day.

Do I need to know anything before starting?

No. We can start with turning the computer on, using the mouse, finding apps, and understanding the screen in front of you.

Want patient one-on-one computer basics help?

If you want someone to sit with you, explain things slowly, and help you practise on your own device, start with our computer lessons for seniors page.
