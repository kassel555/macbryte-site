Video Calls for Seniors

Video calls are one of the best ways to stay close to family, friends, doctors, groups, and community events. This course helps seniors and beginners practise Zoom, FaceTime, WhatsApp, and other video calling tools so joining a call feels familiar instead of stressful.

Choosing and Setting Up the Right App

  • Understand the difference between Zoom, FaceTime, WhatsApp, Google Meet, Teams, and other common video calling tools.
  • Install or update the app you need, sign in when required, and find the safest way to open it again later.
  • Grant camera, microphone, notification, and contact permissions only when they make sense.

Joining a Call From a Link or Invitation

  • Practise joining from an email link, text message, calendar invitation, meeting ID, passcode, or saved contact.
  • Learn what waiting rooms, meeting names, host controls, and join prompts mean before you click through them.
  • Know what to do if the link will not open, the wrong app launches, the passcode is missing, or the call starts in the browser.

Camera, Microphone, and Speaker Confidence

  • Turn camera and microphone on or off, mute and unmute, switch speakers, adjust volume, and test whether others can hear you.
  • Position the device, improve lighting, reduce background noise, and choose a comfortable setup for family calls or appointments.
  • Troubleshoot common problems such as no sound, frozen video, echo, low battery, weak Wi‑Fi, or a camera permission warning.

Hosting and Managing Simple Calls

  • Start a call yourself, invite one person or a small group, and understand what the host can control.
  • Use simple features such as chat, reactions, screen sharing, switching cameras, and ending a call properly.
  • Practise the exact steps you need for family calls, book clubs, medical appointments, lessons, church groups, or community meetings.

Privacy and Video Call Safety

  • Recognize suspicious meeting links, unexpected callers, fake tech-support requests, and people asking you to share private information.
  • Understand when to leave a call, decline a call, block someone, or ask the organizer to resend a trusted invitation.
  • Learn simple privacy habits for background visibility, screen sharing, contact lists, and recorded meetings.

What You Should Feel More Confident Doing

Join video calls from links or invitations with more confidence.

Use camera, microphone, speaker, mute, and chat controls calmly.

Start simple family or group calls when you need to.

Recognize unsafe links or suspicious callers before sharing information.
